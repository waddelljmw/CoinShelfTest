How it works
  • The coin keeps its own true type (Nevada Centennial stays a Nevada coin); it just sits in that spot. Saved: the album, the spot, and one new tick otherInSpot.
  • The spot shows the coin with an orange "something else" ring, so you know the 1891 is still missing.
  • If the spot already has a coin, the app first asks where that coin goes (loose, a box, a stack). Never thrown loose silently.
  • Take out of album on a single spot will ask the same "where does it go?" (Loose is picked, one more tap). Your 11-26 note.

How it works
  • The app suggests the coins (year, Proof ticked, mint mark) and the cost split; you check and change them.
  • Matching empty album spots are offered, never filled by themselves; with 2 of the same album it asks which (your 11-24 rule).
  • Each new coin copies the set's Where from, date, seller, purchase receipt, Inherited / Keeping for. New tick fromSet links it back.
  • The set entry is not deleted: it becomes "Broken up" (gone, like Traded), linked to its coins, so cost history stays. One Undo reverses it all.
Push back: I won't trust built-in set contents I haven't checked. The build looks up which coins each set held by year from the U.S. Mint's public facts only. Where it isn't sure, the list starts empty and you pick the coins. GUESS until checked

Your 3 questions

Q1. Does a spot with "something else" count as filled?
Recommend No: the album says 29 of 30, the 1891 stays on want lists, and the token still counts in your totals.
Q2. Cost split when breaking a set?
Recommend equal shares (you change any line; it must add up). Other choice: split by each coin's value (more exact, but values change).
Q3. Break up only part of a set?
Recommend No, the whole set always. Coins you don't place go loose or into the set's box. Simpler, and totals stay right.
